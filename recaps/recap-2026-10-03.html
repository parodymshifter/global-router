Since 1 October

The 1 October recap named one next step: cut the detour the piece rip-up stage adds. Six variants later, the one that worked is a reclaim pass that reroutes the most-detoured nets under fresh prices, accepting some temporary overflow, then lets the hotspot and refinement stages clear it. It shortened every benchmark that reaches zero, but raised newblue1's residue in every form that let it run there. So it now runs only after the standard path ends at 10 overflow or less, and its result is kept only if it is no worse on overflow.

Against the 2008 contest routers

Overflow

Unchanged since 1 October. On par or better on most benchmarks: zero where NTHU-Route 2.0's re-run did not reach it on bigblue2, bigblue3 and newblue5, and smaller residues on newblue1 and newblue4. Behind on bigblue4 (370 vs 256, best 166), newblue7 (108 vs 68) and newblue3 (38,704 vs about 31,000). newblue1 is still the one benchmark another router takes to zero and we do not (LMgr 0, ours 48).

Wirelength

On the benchmarks that reach zero, the gap is nearly closed. Eight of eleven are within 1% of NTHU-Route 2.0, adaptec3 is under it, and the largest gaps (bigblue1 and bigblue2, +2.3%) are about the size of run-to-run noise. The remaining wirelength gap now sits on the residue benchmarks, where the reclaim does not run: bigblue4 +9.0%, newblue4 +6.0%, newblue1 +4.9%.

Runtime

Unchanged in shape. Faster than NTHU-Route 2.0's re-run on newblue7 (19.6 h vs 24.9 h); 1.5 to 20 times slower on the others before accounting for the faster machine. The reclaim adds 5–10% where it runs and nothing where it does not.

Design

The pipeline is still NTHU-Route 2.0's at its core. New since 1 October, and not in any contest router we know of: a detour reclaim that deliberately accepts temporary overflow to shorten the worst-detoured nets, guarded by running both paths from a saved state and keeping the reclaim only when it is no worse on overflow. The guard makes a quality-risky pass safe to leave on everywhere.

What the results point to

The detour trace is complete, and bigblue4 carries the most

The trace that was still running on 1 October finished. bigblue4 follows the same shape as the others through the piece rip-up stage, but its hotspot box is small (9,887 nets), so that stage removes almost none of the detour. It ends at +14.65% over the pins' minimum spanning tree, the most of any benchmark measured, and is also the largest wirelength gap to NTHU-Route 2.0 (+9.0%).

Shorter routes needed a temporary rise in overflow

The version that kept only reroutes adding no overflow lost most of the gain (bigblue2 −1.2% instead of −4.1%, both against the mean of three baseline runs). The shorter routes mostly pass through full edges, and the gain comes from the renegotiation that clears them afterward. That renegotiation succeeds wherever overflow can reach zero and fails on newblue1, which ended higher in most runs, whether the reclaim avoided its hotspot or ran inside the piece rip-up stage. The fix that worked did not change the reclaim at all; it changed when the result is kept.

The wirelength gap moved to the residue benchmarks

On the 11 zero-overflow benchmarks the total is now 0.84% over NTHU-Route 2.0, inside the run-to-run noise of most of them. The three largest remaining gaps, bigblue4, newblue4 and newblue1, all end with overflow and so never get the reclaim. A reclaim those benchmarks can keep would need either a smaller residue first, or a reclaim that does not disturb an unresolved hotspot, which the avoid-the-hotspot variant showed is harder than keeping its reroutes away from it.

newblue1's residue is a fragile balance

Every change that started a renegotiation on newblue1 left it higher in most runs (8 of 9 at or above the top of its usual range), and its own noise is wider than the first recap reported: six near-neutral runs range from 44 to 54, not 46 to 48. That makes it a poor benchmark for judging small overflow gains, and suggests its last 48 need a different kind of mechanism, as the first recap guessed.

Still to test, in recommended order

Item 1 of the first recap is done in one of its two forms (take detour back afterward). The other form, adding less detour in the first place, is still open and now matters mostly for the residue benchmarks.

  1. newblue1's last 48: virtual capacity. FastRoute 3.0's mechanism, pricing against a lowered capacity inside the hotspot. Under 2 h per run, and a smaller residue would also let the reclaim run there. suggested next
  2. A separate capacity limit for long nets. A second price that pushes long nets out of a contested edge before local nets feel it. Untried, and apparently not in any contest router; still the best candidate for the project's novel contribution.
  3. Add less detour in the piece rip-up stage. For the residue benchmarks, which keep their full detour (bigblue4 +14.65%). Moved up from the first recap's item 1.
  4. One hotspot box per cluster, for newblue7. Scoped; costs a ~9 h checkpoint run before any variant can be tested. scoped
  5. Fewer bends. On adaptec1 the gap to NTHU-Route 2.0 was all vias from more bends; now +0.8% overall, so smaller upside than on 1 October.
  6. Plateau stop for the piece rip-up stage. A newblue3-only runtime gain.
  7. Starting prices from a congestion estimate. Probably low value.

Roadmap steps

  • Step 3, ISPD 2024 format: unchanged. Verifier done and cross-checked; next is the router reading the format.
  • Step 4, multithreading; step 5, GPU maze search and the CUDA CI job; step 6, OpenROAD. Unchanged; see below.

Where multithreading fits

  • Unchanged: almost all runtime is maze searches in the piece rip-up, hotspot and refinement stages; the plan is still to route groups of nets with non-overlapping boxes on all cores against one frozen price snapshot, at an expected 30–50% efficiency.
  • New natural fit: the fallback's two paths start from the same saved state and never touch each other, so they can run on two cores at once. That would remove the reclaim's 5–10% runtime cost on zero-overflow benchmarks with no change to any result.
  • Timing: the detour work changed a stage after the piece rip-up stage, not the stage itself, so the rule against parallelizing a changing stage still applies only if item 3 above goes ahead.

Where OpenROAD fits now

Unchanged since 1 October. It is the evaluator for the ISPD 2025 format, which shares the 2024 inputs, so the 2024 reader comes first. It answers whether a route can actually be detail-routed cleanly, and supplies a FastRoute-derived baseline and OpenSTA timing. First step, when it comes: build the contest's forked OpenROAD and score a trivial solution on one ISPD 2025 case. Order: after the 2024 router works and after multithreading.
