Global Router Recap

Since recap 3 the router learned the ISPD 2024 contest format and was measured against the contest's published results. On raw score it now matches or beats the contest's best team on most of the hidden benchmarks it can fit in memory. Two new mechanisms did most of that: choosing each pin's access point, and a joint 3D pattern reroute after layer assignment. The same reroute shortens wire on every ISPD 2008 benchmark too.

Updated 8 October: after four rounds of memory work, mempool_cluster_ranking (10.6M nets, the largest hidden design we have) now routes, and its score is 0.25% below the contest's #1. It took 16.9 hours on one core, against the winners' 2–5 minutes. Where that time goes and how to cut it: Global Router Speed and Theory.

Since 6 October

  1. Part 102, a format bridge. The 2024 .cap/.net files converted to a 2008 .gr, routed by the unchanged standard, converted back. First legal 2024 result: mempool_tile_rank 14.27M, with the official evaluator agreeing to every digit. Reserving M2 capacity for pin vias made it 17–86% worse and was retired.
  2. Part 103, the native reader. g24_io.h and pd33route read the 2024 files directly, reproducing the bridge exactly. Two additions: via demand charged in layer assignment, and layer assignment priced by the 2024 score itself (−1.0%).
  3. Part 104, the contest designs. All but tera_cluster downloaded. The first design with macros exposed a bug: layers blocked in most GCells were never offered to layer assignment. Fixed; ariane_68_rank 31.9M → 23.6M.
  4. Part 105, the gap analysis. A new tool showed no detours and near-optimal trees. The wirelength gap was access-point choice, and the rest was overflow cost. The winner's access-point rule, adopted: −0.9% to −3.6%.
  5. Part 106, score-priced 2D routing. No effect (±0.02%), because wire length decides almost every pattern choice. Retired.
  6. Part 107, the joint 3D pattern reroute. After layer assignment, each net's pieces may flip their L shape or straighten, with shape and layers chosen together at the exact score. −0.6% to −0.9%. Plus the memory fixes.
  7. Part 108, the same pass on ISPD 2008, under the 2008 cost: shorter wire on all 16 benchmarks.
  8. Part 109, the remaining designs. mempool_group_rank 0.03% below #1; every public design that fit below the best beta submission; the cluster designs ran out of memory.
  9. Parts 110–113, memory (8 October update). Delta snapshots in place of full copies, the fallback's spare state spilled to disk, the netlist streamed in and out, 16-bit route records, and freed memory returned to the OS. Every output byte-identical.
  10. Part 114, mempool_cluster_ranking. Legal, 1,777,655,067, 0.25% below contest #1; official evaluator and our verifier agree to every digit. 16.9 h, peak 29.4 GB.

Where the gap was

Not detours, not topology

2- and 3-pin nets were routed at exactly their bounding box (ratio 1.000), and multi-pin trees came within 0.2–0.8% of an iterated 1-Steiner reference. The tree-building side was already near its limit.

Access points were the wirelength gap

The contest winner's ariane wirelength was below our near-optimal tree over the first access points. Only choosing different access points explains that. The winner's rule (InstantGR, read from its public source) closed it: wirelength now within 0.3–0.5% of #1, and vias below #1 on every design.

Overflow cost is mostly utilisation

The 2024 score charges every busy GCell, not only overflowed ones. On ariane only ~0.12M of 0.96M avoidable overflow cost sat on GCells over capacity. What paid it down was choosing each piece's L orientation together with its layers. Pricing the 2D stage differently and re-pricing layers on fixed routes did not.

Memory

mempool_group_rank first ran out of memory: a 26 GB footprint on this 24 GB machine. A 5-second profile of bsg_chip_rank showed the real cause, which wasn't the first guess. Layer assignment's per-edge state across 10 layers roughly tripled the footprint the moment it started:

  • an empty history undo log per wire edge, 24 bytes each on tens of millions of edges, only a few of them ever used: now stored only where it exists;
  • via-site prices that NTHU-mode layer assignment never reads: no longer allocated;
  • a tree per net held for every net at once: now rebuilt per net from its 2D route.

Routes are byte-identical, and the peak on bsg_chip_rank fell 26%. mempool_group_rank then finished at a 22.9 GB peak, inside the machine. The mempool_cluster designs (10.6M nets) do not fit yet. Their pattern stage completes at about 20–24 GB, but entering the first maze stage pushes the footprint past 28 GB, where a watchdog stops the run rather than let it thrash (stopped at 30.7 GB and 28.7 GB). Both were heavily congested at that point: 2D overflow 765K and 524K after the pattern stage.

8 October update (Parts 110–113). Four rounds of changes, each checked byte for byte against the previous outputs: delta snapshots in place of full route copies; the fallback's spare state spilled to disk on designs above 2M nets; the netlist streamed in and the solution streamed out instead of held whole; 16-bit route records; narrower layer-assignment state; and freed memory handed back to the OS. Peaks fell to 0.28 GB on mempool_tile_rank (was 0.75), 0.51 GB on ariane_68_rank (was 1.77) and 4.98 GB on bsg_chip_rank (was 7.39, measured before the last three rounds).

mempool_cluster_ranking then ran end to end. The stage that stopped the earlier attempt at 30.7 GB peaked at 20.1 GB. The overall peak, 29.4 GB, comes from layer assignment, which adds 10.8 GB in its first round; most of it sat in compressed memory and swap without slowing the run.

Caveats

  • Runtime. The contest ranked a runtime-scaled score on 8 cores plus 4 A100 GPUs, where the winners took 1–50 seconds per design up to mempool_group, 2–5 minutes on mempool_cluster and 23–34 minutes on tera_cluster. Ours runs on one CPU core: 2–26 minutes on the small and medium designs, 2.7 hours on mempool_group_rank, 16.9 hours on mempool_cluster_ranking. On the contest's own scaled ranking we would place far lower.
  • Hidden-benchmark tuning. All settings come from one design (mempool_tile_rank) and the gap analysis. Nothing was tuned per design, and the rank and public versions use identical settings.
  • Margins. On nvdla_rank and mempool_group_rank the margins over #1 are 0.007% and 0.03%, effectively ties.
