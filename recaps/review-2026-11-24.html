Why the 2025 loop is where the room is

Refinement is a fixed-point iteration: measure slack on the best route, tighten classes, reroute, repeat. It has the shape of a Lagrangian method -- the classes are coarse multipliers on each net's timing constraint -- but with only half the update: a multiplier rises when a net fails timing and never falls when a net passes with room to spare. The congestion side of this project learned the same lesson twice: the ever-rising lambda price gave way to present-demand pricing, which falls back as demand leaves an edge (Parts 59-60, together with keeping every reroute), and the user's own LIFO-relaxed history beat the permanent one (kroute, CLAUDE.md's 2026-09-07 update). The timing side has not had that change. Evidence that it matters: classed nets only grow (blind mempool_group 337803 at round 3 → 373381 at round 24 of the patience run), every classed net pays congestion, and the loop was still improving 0.001-0.004 per round in its last rounds.

The other half is cost. A round reroutes all nets though only the newly classed ones changed, and scores each route twice (F7). Cheaper rounds are what make a longer, properly relaxing iteration affordable on the large designs.
