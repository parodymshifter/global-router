Global Router Recap

Four days, all on ISPD 2025. A delay model replaced the resistance-only timing bias, and the loop's ladder grew upward where the timing strength tops out. The Elmore stage (21 November, standard)

The timing bias had pulled critical nets toward low-resistance layers. A delay model says otherwise: a micron of wire on layer l adds about Rdrv·Cl + Rl·Cdown to the net's delay, and with NanGate45's values the driver term dominates, so metal4-8 (the highest capacitance) are the slow layers, not metal2. gr/elmore_rows.py turns that into a per-net, per-layer cost; after refinement the loop reroutes its best at K 35 / 70 / 140 and keeps the best.

Refinement's stop rule (22-24 November, on test)

Refining again from the Elmore routes gained on the two designs whose classic refinement had stopped at one bad round (visible mempool_tile_wrap −1.4049 → −1.6478, blind mempool_group 1.0946 → 0.9442), and almost nothing elsewhere. A control run showed why: continuing classic refinement past its stop gave mempool_tile_wrap the same. So the lever was the stop rule. timing_loop.py --patience 3 lets refinement go on until four rounds in a row bring no new best, and keeps the best; it was tested with a cap of 24 rounds on nine designs, full loops from scratch:

Patience never makes a design worse: the best is still the minimum, and its rounds come after the standard's stop. Its cost is time: blind mempool_group ran 16.5 h of refinement. Measurement

OpenROAD's power reading is not quite repeatable. On mempool_tile_wrap, 5 of 35 slack sessions read a route's power 4e-5 above its scoring session (TNS and WNS exact; the same route re-measured alone gave the scored value three times; the cause is not found). The loop's re-measure check, there to catch a different route, stopped two runs on it; it now allows 1e-4 relative on power, logs the difference and keeps the scored value, also in scores.jsonl for --resume. The noise is worth 0.0018 of score change on that design: gains smaller than that are not gains.

Code and model review

Full page: review-2026-11-24.html. The headlines:

fix first CI checks the previous router

The static analysis, sanitizer, Valgrind and timing jobs build pd38route; nothing has checked pd39route that way since 14 October. Locally it is clean: 0 warnings, 0 sanitizer reports, 2 low analyzer warnings, 88% line coverage.

worth doing A self-test that never reaches its code; 180 settings with no typo guard

The fixed-topology slow-tier test passes with its target at 0% coverage. A misspelled setting name runs the default silently. A settings registry would fix the second and make 118 settings no standard file sets easy to retire.

the room The 2025 loop

Each refinement round is a full reroute plus two OpenROAD sessions on the same route; one session would save about 18 of every ~40 OpenROAD minutes. Its classes only ever tighten -- half a Lagrangian update, the half the congestion side of this project dropped long ago. Timing multipliers that fall as well as rise, and warm-started rounds, are untried.

Process

Mistakes this period, each corrected on the record: a claim that blind mempool_group had never been tried above K 140 (it had, on 21 November); a count of "5 of 17" slack sessions with a power difference (5 of 35); a wait in the baseline job that matched no process, so it started beside the patience run for six minutes (routes are deterministic, only that round's time was affected), and the pkill that stopped it also stopped a watcher; a watcher whose last cut buffered its output, so sessions went unreported for half an hour; a guess of "20 minutes in" for an analyzer that had finished in four.
