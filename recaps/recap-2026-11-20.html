Global Router Recap

Eleven days, three turns. A two-ended search made the ISPD 2008 suite three times faster. A rule audit found that this router had been routing nets the contest exempts, and leaving them out moved more overflow than any late-stage mechanism ever had: newblue1 reached zero, bigblue4 fell to 90 against a published 160. On ISPD 2025 a bug in our own slack measurement had hidden the worst timing paths of one design; fixed, that design's contest score fell by 70%, and our blind six now sum below first place's.

The exempt nets

ISPD 2008 does not require nets with more than 1000 pins to be routed; both scorers skip a missing route for them, and NTHU-Route 2.0 leaves them out. This router had routed them since its first generation; Part 36 even found them the worst overflow offenders and made them negotiate. Comparing NTHU-Route's bigblue4 routes with ours net by net found it (Part 212). With PD39ROUTE_SKIP_GIANT=1, now in fast08.env:

The lesson, bigger than any algorithm this project has tuned: audit the model against the contest's rules before tuning. The 2024 and 2025 rules were audited the same way afterwards (Part 214): no optimization mismatch there, but our stage report counted the idle-GCell floor twice; fixed, it agrees with the evaluators within 0.3%, routes byte-identical.

A second comparison with NTHU-Route 2.0's last phase, from the same routes, found that its refinement keeps every reroute while ours reverted half of them (NTHU −7550, ours −308). Keeping them above 1000 units of overflow (PD39ROUTE_REFINE_KEEP_ABOVE=1000, Part 213) took newblue3 from 33318 to 31434; below 1000 it hurt the small residues, so the rule switches on there.

How much is forced

Two new exact tools (Parts 208, 213) solve the routing problem around a hotspot as a linear program over generated candidate routes (column generation, HiGHS). gr/hotspot_flow.py turns that into a repair: solve, round, and run the late stages from the rounded routes.

  • newblue7: its main hotspot's fractional optimum is exactly 54, which our 54 there already meets; the other 2 units sit on one edge whose fractional optimum is 0 but every rounding, and a 5000-net exact re-solve, ends at 2. Closed at 56.
  • newblue3: the fractional optimum lies between 26264 and 32141; the three isolated hotspots already sit exactly at their cut bounds.
  • bigblue4: purpose-made candidates showed a fractional optimum near 196 with the exempt nets routed (Part 208, from 318); with them out, the flow reached 90.

Runtime

Late searches flooded their boxes from the source end: the sink pin sits in a small, expensive pocket, so 99.7% of a median box is cheaper to reach from the sources than the route found (Part 196). A search from both ends at once, exact in cost (PD39ROUTE_SEARCH_BIDIR=2, in speed08.env):

So residues run as a portfolio over six slow-tier orderings (gr/reorder_portfolio.py, Part 202), keeping the best by overflow, max overflow and wire length: 1.45× the one-ended time, and a small gain. A search quota for slow-tier batches (Parts 193–195) helped some designs and hurt newblue3; not adopted. Neither change pays on 2024 or 2025, whose slow tier does more work two-ended (Part 200).

ISPD 2025: a measurement bug, and the winners' scores

Scored as the contest scored them (Part 215, from the contest's published weights, endpoint counts and the blind round's reference medians). Our endpoint counts differ from the contest's on the mempool designs; no pick changes.

The bug. The loop classes critical nets from the slack OpenROAD measures on failing paths. On blind mempool_tile_wrap the script dropped every path containing one point it could not name: 6278 of about 7783 failing paths, the worst among them, leaving 665 nets instead of 9263. The narrow, measured classes therefore never moved timing there, and the broad input-slack classes won, at a large congestion cost. Where the bias costs. 95.4% of the overflow cost the timing bias adds lands on metal7–10 (Part 190). Our unbiased routes have lower congestion than both winners on every blind design, but our timing picks add +56% to +83% on the mempool designs. Three ways to aim the bias better were built and scored, and none helped:

  • weighting nets by their benefit (Part 205);
  • weighting nets by their driver's strength (Part 207);
  • delay-aware trees, whose ceiling lay across nets, not along a tree (Part 204).

Wirelength and vias, scoped (Part 214)

Every lever measured on the 2008 wire length is small:

  • a 2D post-pass, about 1% of a window's wire;
  • layer assignment, at most 2.8% of the score: on bigblue1 a certified via lower bound (gr/labound.cpp) is 1807213 against 1965453 used, with capacity respected;
  • excess bends in the 2D shapes, at most about 0.8%.

Measured and set aside

  • Blocker eviction chains on bigblue4 (Part 191): blockers move, but the room never adds up; the neighbourhood is saturated out to 60 tiles.
  • Joint selection over the portfolio's six routings (Part 206): fractional room on two designs, no whole-route mix below the base.
  • Random-walk rounding (gr/dwalk.cpp, Part 213): no gain over the rounding already used.
  • Solved prices fed back into the router (Part 210): seeded newblue1 runs end at 28–30, inside its 22–36 spread; no gain.
  • Turning the region stage off (Part 215): each pricing variant settles at its own level whatever the start; without the stage newblue3 ends at 32278–32404 against 31434.

Process

  • Claims and evidence (18 November): every possible / impossible / absent / tried / forced claim names the check behind it, with its scope, or is labelled unverified. Two claims made without checks prompted it ("the linear-algebra libraries aren't installed": Eigen, OpenBLAS and Accelerate were; "the model audit found no other mismatch": no audit had been run; it then was).
  • Network failures are scoped with the network-triage skill before any report, which names the specific failure.
  • Own-pipeline results only: NTHU-Route's port (GPL v3) and its routes are diagnostics.
  • Measure a diagnosis before building on it (Part 150's "near-tie" was wrong); give a quality-neutral-looking change several reorderings before believing its overflow.
