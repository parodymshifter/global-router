Routing Difficulty Atlas

Pre-routing measurements of every global-routing benchmark this project routes, next to how hard each one turned out to be. Pick measures, see whether the designs cluster, and fit outcomes by least squares. Areas are in GCells (the routing grid's cells); "Steiner" is the rectilinear Steiner minimal tree (RSMT), the shortest right-angle tree joining a net's pins.

What the data say so far

Read each contest on its own: the contests use different grids, layer counts and scoring rules, so correlations pooled across them mostly sort designs by contest. Samples are small (16 ISPD 2008 designs, 13 ISPD 2024, 10 ISPD 2025): with 16 designs a rank correlation needs to reach about ±0.5 to mean anything, and among some 60 measures a few will reach it by chance.

  • ISPD 2008 overflow follows local bottlenecks, not overall density. The worst 3 x 3-GCell cut (Spearman +0.75) and the worst pin escape (+0.63) track the remaining overflow; pin density barely does. The cut bounds agree: at least 26264 of newblue3's overflow is forced for any router, and newblue4's residue sits at 98% of its tightest cut. They rank the designs but do not predict the size of their overflow: eleven designs sit at zero and newblue3 at 38948, and the least-squares fit below scores a leave-one-out R² below zero.
  • Detour follows pin density. ISPD 2008 detour against pins per track: +0.77.
  • RUDY works where it should. ISPD 2024: the share of GCells over capacity against RUDY's 99th percentile edge, +0.87 — the classic congestion estimate's best showing here.
  • The clusters are contests and sizes, with one exception. Principal components separate overall density (about 36% of the variation), size (22%) and local bottlenecks (12.5%); three k-means clusters are ISPD 2008, the smaller 2024 / 2025 designs and the large mempool designs. At four, newblue3 splits off alone — the design no published router brings near zero.
  • Per-net route time (thread CPU time, re-measured): on ISPD 2024 / 2025 a net costs 8–12 µs per Steiner step and its length explains its time (log correlation 0.93–0.97). On ISPD 2008 the maze search takes 74–100% of per-net time, the slowest 1% of nets take 47–96% of it, and length explains far less (0.64–0.90): the hard nets, not the many, set the runtime.
  • ISPD 2025 timing. A design's timing difficulty is largely set before routing: the input pin slack predicts the routed slack (correlation 0.95 on ariane). What routing controls is the margin, and the timing loop's gain over the unbiased route ranges from 0.2 to 7.5 score points across the ten designs.

Any two measures

Each point is one design. The numbers under the chart are Spearman rank correlations (from -1 to 1; 0 means no ordering relation), for all designs and within each contest.

Do the designs cluster?

The selected measures are standardized (each to mean 0, standard deviation 1) and projected onto their two principal components: the two directions along which the designs differ most. Shaded outlines are k-means clusters, computed on all selected measures, not just the two plotted.

Which measures track which outcomes

Spearman rank correlation between each pre-routing measure and each routing outcome. Outcomes from different contests are not on one scale, so the contest filter matters: it starts on one contest, and "All" pools ranks across contests, which mixes different scoring rules and mostly sorts designs by contest.

Least-squares fit

Ridge regression (least squares with a penalty that shrinks coefficients toward zero; penalty 0 is ordinary least squares) on standardized measures. The leave-one-out score refits the model once per design without it and predicts it: with this few designs, that number is the honest one. Keep the measures few.
