Global Router Recap

Seventeen days, three threads. On ISPD 2008 a cost that matches the contest's own objective took the hardest benchmark from 38898 to 33492 units of overflow, and a three-variant portfolio brought four of the five residues to their best yet; a faster search queue and a batched, gated region stage cut the long runs by half or more. On ISPD 2025 all twelve designs now have a timing score, mempool_cluster included, routed and scored on a 24 GB laptop at 80–113 GB of memory footprint. And one measurement reframes the 2024 / 2025 overflow term: most of it is a floor no route can go below.

The over-capacity cost

The ISPD 2008 score charges every unit beyond capacity the same, wherever it lands. The router's present-demand cost, hist × cong5, charges an edge 50 units over far more than a just-full one. On newblue3, where at least 26264 units are forced, that kept pushing nets off edges that stay over capacity whatever happens, onto detours that overflowed other edges. Part 179 let the region stage read demand capped at capacity plus a slack (PD39ROUTE_REGION_OVER). The results, as portfolio variants beside the standard path:

ISPD 2025: every design scored with timing

Sticky re-classification (Part 188). Classing nets from one biased route's slack dropped the nets the bias had fixed. The loop now measures its best route's own failing-path slack, merges it per net with every slack measured before (keeping the worst), and reroutes while that gains: ten designs −23.97 → −25.11, none worse. A third round gave −25.19 and was not adopted.

mempool_cluster (Part 189), 10–11 million nets, was the last design without a timing score. One OpenROAD session peaks at 80–113 GB of memory footprint on this 24 GB machine, almost all of it in swap. Three changes made it fit: --lean compresses every route that can no longer win; SLACK_BANDS=16 walks the failing paths in slack bands, with slack files byte-identical to a single walk; and --resume appends every score to scores.jsonl as it is measured. The two clusters differ in one place that shows why no fixed rule works: the power term won on the blind design and lost on the visible one. Both cut power by 0.6–0.7 W for a similar rise in overflow, but the blind design weights power 2 and the visible 0.3, so the same cut was worth −1.40 score units on one and −0.18 on the other.

Two lessons from the cluster runs

  • Excluding a folder from Time Machine keeps it out of backups, not out of local snapshots. A snapshot taken while an 11–12 GB route was uncompressed kept its space after --lean compressed it. Free disk fell to the guard's floor and stopped the blind loop at 06:33 on 8 November. Before a disk-tight run, pause automatic backups.
  • Refine can reproduce its own route. The blind loop's second refine round routed a file byte-identical to the first round's and still scored it for 1 h 35 min. A comparison before scoring would have skipped it.

The idle floor of the overflow term

The 2024 / 2025 overflow cost charges every GCell with capacity c the amount exp(0.5 (d/2 − c)), so an empty GCell still pays exp(−c/2). Summed over the die (p189/g24_floor, from the .cap alone; it reproduces the recorded 2024 floors to the digit), that floor is a cost no route can go below:

  • 54–97% of the unbiased route's overflow cost on all 25 designs: 83–94% on most 2025 designs, 54–76% on the mempool group and cluster designs.
  • 95.5–95.6% of the mempool_cluster floor sits on metal7–10, the wide upper layers with few tracks per GCell.
  • On the twelve 2025 designs the part above the floor, summed, is 3.43 score units, while the best timing routes add more overflow cost than that. The overflow term matters through how much the timing bias adds, so a cheaper way to carry the critical nets is the lever, not a better congestion stage.

Open the Atlas v7 (in this recap). What it adds:

  • The changeable overflow is predictable before routing. The cost above the floor per GCell ranks with RUDY's 99th-percentile edge at Spearman +0.94 over the 25 designs of 2024 and 2025.
  • The timing loop's gain is not. It ranges from 0.24 to 7.52 score points across the twelve designs, and no pre-routing measure ranks it: the strongest reaches |0.42|, noise at n = 12.
  • The spectrum measures stay in as candidates; Part 139 found they add no reliable predictive power.

Process

  • Strict readers (Part 155): out-of-spec input now stops the run, naming the file and line. Exercising the verification principle on the readers found many silent repairs; gr/input_check reads a design without routing it.
  • The forced-overflow report (Part 162): every run that ends with overflow now groups it into hotspots and says how much of each the cut bound certifies as forced. This is the "return to placement" exit, as information.
  • Every new setting gets an end-to-end check that runs the router and looks for evidence the mechanism acted (gr/e2e_settings.py, in CI).
  • Long loops checkpoint to disk (scores.jsonl, --resume), and temporary files live in one folder excluded from backups (~/Code/GlobalRouterTemp), with the snapshot caveat above.

From this project's own findings

1. Check NTHU-Route 2.0's posted routes

Score their bigblue4 (160), newblue4 (138) and newblue7 (54) with gr_verify and eval2008.pl, then compare their bigblue4 hotspot with ours (x 204–212, y 132–148).

Whether newblue7 is already solved to its bound, and what a 2× better bigblue4 does differently. Minutes of work, but it needs your permission to download their files.

2. Break the timing bias's added overflow down by layer

The contest evaluator already prints overflow cost per layer. Re-evaluating each design's best and unbiased routes shows where the bias spends overflow: the floor says metal7–10 have few tracks per GCell, and the bias pulls critical nets toward those low-resistance layers.

This tells us whether a layer-aware bias (moderately critical nets kept on the mid layers, with more tracks) could carry them for less. Cheap: no OpenROAD, minutes per design.

3. Skip scoring a refine route identical to the best

Byte-compare before the OpenROAD session.

Saves 1.5 h on a mempool_cluster loop; a few lines in timing_loop.py plus a self-test.

4. Make room by rerouting blocking legal nets, supervised

Part 151's options 2–5: rip up legal nets that hold the cheap way through a hotspot, reroute them, and keep the move only if overflow does not rise. Lateral shifts (option 1) failed because the parallel rows are full.

The direct test for newblue1, which is not cut-bound. Medium effort.

5. Make distance matter again in late region searches

A wire-length floor on the heuristic gave −5–8% search work alone (Part 138); a ring bound around the sink (Part 150) is untried.

Runtime on the residues. Small to medium; judged on overflow, since ties break differently.

6. Exact per-round savings in the region stage (plan A4)

Keep the best state as a delta, and track overflow incrementally instead of rescanning the grid.

Byte-identical by construction; the size is unknown until profiled on newblue7.

7. Compare with the 2025 winners' raw scores (plan D4)

Our numbers are changes against our own unbiased routes; only ariane has been set against 1st place (0.14 vs 0.90).

Needed before any claim about where the twelve stand. Work: the contest's leaderboard and reference medians.

From published research and contest publications

8. Performance-driven layer assignment (GAP-LA)

Zhao, Guo, Zhang and Lin (Peking University; Zhao and Lin are the 2025 contest's winning team, Hippo) assign layers by a tree dynamic program with Elmore delay and a look-ahead, with timing-critical nets picked from static timing analysis. They report 8.5–18.3% better WNS and 2.0–5.1% better TNS than the winners' original flow on the same 2D topology.

Fits option 2: our timing bias acts on the whole route; this acts only on layers, where the overflow cost is.

arXiv 2507.13375

9. Gradient-guided RC weighting (ISPD 2026)

Xiao, Duan, Jin, Liu, Ho, Young and Wong (CUHK / HKBU) take each wire segment's capacitance and resistance sensitivity from timing gradients and use them as weights. They report 14.3% better WNS and 18.5% better TNS than the 2025 winner, and 10.6% lower switching power with the power term.

Our classes are per net, in slack bands. Per-segment sensitivity would bias only the segments that matter, which should add less overflow.

HKBU listing · paper

10. An LP / column-generation post-pass for wire length (GRIP; plan B5)

Wu, Davoodi and Linderoth price candidate routes with a linear program over rectangular subregions, then minimize overflow in a second phase. They report 9.23% better wire length and 5.24% better via cost than the best published results on the 2007 / 2008 sets.

The eleven zero-overflow designs trail NTHU-Route 2.0 by up to 2.3%, and reclaim budgets are saturated. Builds on gr/lpbound.cpp. Large effort.

paper

11. Baselines: OpenROAD's own router and InstantGR 2.0

OpenROAD's FastRoute-based grt favours its worst-slack nets through -critical_nets_percentage, a direct baseline for our timing loop. InstantGR 2.0 (TCAD, January 2026), a GPU 3D router, reports beating the 2024 contest winner in runtime and quality.
